My role

I designed Perfect Properties in Figma: the information architecture, the main user flows, the screens and their states, and the UI kit of colors, type and components the screens are built from.

What I did

  • Mapped the app into five areas: get started, find, decide, finance and global menu
  • Designed 27 mobile screens plus overlays for the menu, sharing, contacting an agent and notifications
  • Built a UI kit with buttons and their states, inputs, sliders, progress bar, checkboxes, dropdowns, cards, icons, and the iOS status bar, keyboards, sharesheet and navigation bar
  • Defined a type scale in Raleway with an Acme wordmark, and a brown and tan palette

The problem

Looking for a home means juggling many small jobs: searching an area, narrowing results, keeping track of favorites, comparing two places you like, getting in touch with an agent, and working out whether you can afford it.

When each of those jobs lives in a different place, the search feels heavy. I wanted one app where every step leads naturally to the next, and where the look feels warm and calm rather than busy.

Goals

  • Personal from the start: a short onboarding that shapes the first search
  • Easy to narrow: filters, chips and a map that share one search
  • Easy to decide: key facts first, and side-by-side comparison
  • Easy to act: tour, agent and financing one tap away from a listing
  • Consistent: one UI kit across every screen

Who it’s for

The screens point to three kinds of people, all on their phones:

  • Home buyers searching an area, saving favorites and booking tours
  • Renters using the same search with the Rent filter
  • Buyers who need a loan and want to know where to start with a mortgage

Key design decisions

Each part of the app answers one job in the search. Click any screen to open it in the interactive walkthrough.

01

Welcome slides that tell the story in four words

The first screens explain the whole product with four short words: Search, Save, See, Sold. Each slide has one photo and one line. Set up Profile and I’ll do it later stay in the same spot on every slide, so people can start or skip at any time.

02

Onboarding you can skip, step by step

Four short steps ask what kind of home you want, your price and room count, where you want to live and which features you need. A numbered progress bar shows how far along you are, every step has Skip this step, and answers are big chips and pills that need one tap. The answers set up the search that follows.

03

One search, as a list or a map

Search remembers your current location and recent searches. Results share one header across views: the search bar, a Filter button and quick chips. A floating bar keeps Save search and Map close to the thumb, and the full filter sheet covers buy, rent or sold, price, size, rooms, year built and layout.

04

Side-by-side comparison

Choosing between two homes usually means jumping back and forth. Compare mode adds a checkbox to each listing, then lines the two homes up in one table: lot size, price, beds, baths, year built, layout and public transport.

05

A listing page built around the next step

The detail page starts with photos, price and the three facts people scan first: beds, baths and size. A map, the overview and highlights follow. A sticky bar keeps the monthly estimate and Schedule tour in view while you scroll. From there you can contact the agent, chat to set up a tour, or share the home by text, email or the native share sheet.

06

Financing in plain, short steps

Home loans are explained in three easy steps before any form appears. The questions use one-tap answers and the same progress bar as onboarding, so the pattern already feels familiar. A clear confirmation says what happens next.

07

Saved homes that keep working for you

Saved properties sit on Home next to new listings, and a push notification flags when a saved home drops in price, so the app brings people back at the right moment.

Type

Raleway carries the whole interface. Acme gives the Perfect Properties wordmark its own friendly voice. The sizes below are the labels on the Typography page.

Components

  • Buttons: primary 351 × 56, secondary 95 × 34, text button. States are Enabled, Hovered, Pressed and Disabled. The board says to include a drop shadow on each button, and it marks the label Text: Bold, 20px.
  • Inputs: Email, Password, Confirm Password, Search, and a min–max Price pair
  • Controls: slider (1960–2022), numbered progress bar, checkbox (Selected and Un-selected) and dropdown (Collapsed and Expanded)
  • Cards: one property card in Mobile, Tablet and Desktop sizes
  • Icons: the named symbols on the Icons page, listed under the icon sheet
  • Overlays: menu, Share Home dialog, share sheet, Contact Agent, price-drop notification
  • iOS: Sharesheet, Alphabetic Keyboard, Numeric Keyboard, Status Bar, Navigation Bar

Reflection

A shared UI kit is what holds this app together. Buttons, chips, inputs and the property card are defined once, so newer screens such as compare and the mortgage questions use the same parts and feel like they belong.

Reusing patterns helps the people using the app too: the progress bar and one-tap answers from onboarding come back in the mortgage flow, so the second time feels familiar.
